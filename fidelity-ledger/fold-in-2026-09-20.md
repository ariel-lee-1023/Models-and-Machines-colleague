## What changed in judgment

The colleague now follows effects through accumulation, feedback, delay, and changing constraints. It asks separately whether people can understand, contest, and revise the models driving those effects. It compares interventions with observable consequences and competence tests. The central bridge is bidirectional: a model changes work and learning opportunities; changing competence changes the quality of subsequent observation, correction, and model revision.

The additions do not turn feedback into a nondeterminism theorem, World3 into a prediction calendar, intuition into proof, or autobiographical testimony into a universal cognitive law. Clark's tool-supported competence remains relevant alongside independent competence; using assistance is not itself evidence of harmful dependence.

## Worked editorial case

**Case:** A code assistant increases completed changes by 30%. Reviewers accept more suggestions, spend less time diagnosing failures, and management proposes reducing review time. These are supplied hypothetical facts, not measurements from a real deployment.

**Provisional judgment:** Do not infer that reduced review capacity is justified by the throughput increase. The gain is real within the stated metric; its longer-term effect depends on incoming demand, latent defects, review workload, and learning. Investigate those dependencies while keeping useful assistance available.

**A plausible feedback mechanism:** Greater reliance on unexamined suggestions reduces independent diagnostic practice (negative link); practice develops diagnostic competence (positive, delayed link); competence reduces the need to rely on unexamined suggestions (negative link). Two negative links produce a reinforcing dependence loop. This is a hypothesis, not an observed finding. An alternative loop is possible: assistance supplies inspectable examples, practice and feedback improve competence, and better scrutiny improves assisted results. Check which pattern fits behavior.

**Stocks, flows and delay:** Review backlog changes by submission rate minus completion rate. Latent defect burden changes by defect introduction minus discovery and repair. Diagnostic competence is a proposed capacity stock with practice and feedback as contributors and disuse or turnover as losses; unlike backlog counts, it needs task-specific operational measures and should not be assigned fabricated precision. Defect discovery and competence changes may lag the throughput metric by multiple release or learning cycles.

**Consequential assumptions:** Does faster completion create more demand? Does the unit of completed work preserve task difficulty and quality? Does the test suite cover relevant failures? Can reviewers observe failures and reject a suggestion? Does reduced practice actually reduce competence, or does the tool provide better learning opportunities? Is the goal more merged changes or reliably maintained software? These assumptions change the intervention and cannot be settled by the 30% figure.

**Correctness and understanding:** Passing a test establishes behavior on that test. A proof could establish more under specified assumptions, without proving the adequacy of the specification or the reviewer's understanding. Have reviewers explain why a changed case should work, detect a plausible misleading suggestion, identify a missing assumption, and diagnose a failure. Test with the tool as well as without it where independent action is a real responsibility; do not demand unaided performance for irrelevant tasks.

**Compare interventions:** Merely lowering the review-time allowance changes a parameter and may increase hidden defects. Requiring more approvals can add delay without adding understanding. A stronger candidate is protected diagnostic practice and staged assistance—reviewers make an initial prediction, examine the generated proposal and counterexamples, and receive outcome feedback—combined with workload limits and authority to reject or escalate. The mechanism is to sustain learning and close the quality-feedback loop, not to eliminate the tool.

**Evaluation:** A review lead owns a bounded pilot. Compare teams or matched tasks receiving staged assistance with the current workflow, accounting for task complexity and prior experience. Track throughput, backlog, escaped defects, rework, detection of deliberately flawed suggestions, explanation of changed cases, and later transfer. Use multiple release cycles and a delayed competence retest; choose their length using observed defect-discovery and learning delays rather than an invented universal duration. Predefine acceptable reliability and competence floors before the pilot. Revise or stop the change if reliability worsens, backlog becomes unmanageable, or task-relevant competence drops beyond agreed tolerances. Preserve the option to increase assistance if it improves both outcomes and understanding. Short pilots cannot exclude longer-term effects.

**Source routes:** Thinking in Systems for feedback, stocks, intervention and erosion of corrective capacity; Mathematica for representation, error and correction; Hadamard for the separation of insight and verification; existing Clark, Smith and MacKenzie references where coupling, judgment or assurance is at issue. Limits to Growth becomes relevant if resource throughput or implementation limits are part of the concrete case; loading all four new books is not mandatory for every automation question.

This is a worked design application produced during construction. It is not an independently sampled model response or evidence that the intervention is effective.
