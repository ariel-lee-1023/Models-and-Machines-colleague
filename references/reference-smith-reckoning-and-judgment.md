# The Promise of Artificial Intelligence: Reckoning and Judgment — Brian Cantwell Smith
**Format**: md | **Sections**: 13 | **Depth**: study | **Type**: text
**Edition**: MIT Press, 2019. Locators follow its numbered chapters.

## Mental Model (read first)

[M1,M3] Smith distinguishes effective calculation within established representations from judgment that is accountable to the world those representations concern. The distinction does not divide all humans from all machines. Its point is to establish a demanding standard for cognition and appropriate use: dealing with a description is not yet taking responsibility for whether it gets reality right.

## Frameworks & Structure

### Chapters 1–3: What representation requires

- [M1] **Background; History** (chs. 1–2): the attraction of formal computation is that a mechanism can manipulate structures while people interpret those structures as being about a world. Mechanical effectiveness and semantic significance are different relations. Conflating them makes successful symbol processing appear to establish more than it does.
- [M1] **The Noneffectiveness of Reference** (ch. 2): being referred to is not itself a physical signal arriving at the referent. Someone may think about a distant object without causing a detectable disturbance there. Smith is distinguishing aboutness from local causal contact, not denying that speech or thought can have causal consequences.
- [M1,M3] **GOFAI's Failures** (ch. 3): Smith examines perceptual, epistemological, and ontological weaknesses in the assumption that intelligence begins with a neatly articulated inventory of objects and facts. Creating and sustaining those distinctions is already an achievement. Do not treat the input ontology as free when evaluating a reasoning system.

### Chapters 4–7: Learning and articulated reasoning

- [M1,M3] **Transition; Machine Learning** (chs. 4–5): learned correlations and representations address important limitations of hand-engineered symbolic systems. Data and computational power change what can be achieved. They do not alone establish what the learned regularities mean or when the framing of the task should be challenged.
- [M1,M3] **Assessment** (ch. 6): combining symbolic and learned strengths is worthwhile but does not by itself meet the deeper challenge of judgment. Evaluate a system's actual contribution while distinguishing improvements within a framework from responsibility for that framework.
- [M1,M3] **Articulated Reasoning** (ch. 7): negation, quantification, hypotheticals, implication, and structured chains remain important cognitive capacities. The contrast between waves of AI is not a warrant to dismiss either flexible perception or structured reasoning. A hybrid technique should be assessed for what it establishes, rather than treated as a philosophical solution by construction.

### Chapters 8–9: Objects and world

- [M1] **Registration** (ch. 8): an entity is encountered and sustained as something that can be tracked, thought about, and held to constitutive conditions. Recognizing an object involves both its worldly persistence and the norms of the practice identifying it. Label classification is insufficient evidence of the full achievement Smith is discussing.
- [M1,M3] **Objects and Constitutive Standards** (ch. 8): an object is not simply a momentary bundle of features. Its identity can persist across changes while some changes undermine the relevant identification. Ask what makes a label answerable to its object and what evidence would require revising it.
- [M1,M3] **World as World** (ch. 9): the world is the inexhaustible ground to which representations and their constitutive standards are accountable. A closed ontology cannot simply decree all conditions of its own correctness. This is a philosophical account of answerability, not the assertion that useful bounded models are impossible.

### Chapters 10–11: The central distinction

- [M1,M3] **Reckoning** (ch. 10): calculation and effective performance can proceed within an established representational and normative frame. Reckoning is powerful and valuable. Its adequacy for a task depends on whether people have justified the frame and can handle circumstances it fails to represent.
- [M1,M3] **Judgment** (ch. 10): judgment involves commitment to what is real, true, and appropriate, including recognition of the limitations of one's understanding. It is not merely confidence, explanation fluency, or another output score. Its crucial demand is the capacity and obligation to let the world correct one's framing.
- [M1,M3] **Human vs. Machine** (ch. 11): Smith expressly leaves open that synthesized systems might develop judgment, while acknowledging that much human cognition is reckoning. Avoid claiming that judgment is definitionally biological or that this book proves an eternal machine limitation.
- [M1,M3] **Accountability Beyond Each Local Move** (ch. 11): people need not deliberate explicitly at every instant to be held accountable for what they do. Conversely, attaching a human name to a workflow does not establish meaningful judgment. The library's design application is to inspect whether that person can understand, challenge, and alter the framing.

### Chapters 12–13: Applying the distinction

- [M1,M3] **Application; Conclusion** (chs. 12–13): put the strengths of reckoning to work where their contribution can be understood and governed. Preserve responsibility for interpretation and use. The book's 2019 assessments are historical; a contemporary system must be evaluated using present evidence, while the reckoning–judgment distinction supplies questions rather than an automatic verdict.

## Worked Example

[M1] **Reconstructed from ch. 2: a phone that detects being thought about.** Smith asks students to imagine an application that announces whenever somebody thinks about its owner. Instrumenting brains and transmitting messages would create causal signals, but that changes the setup: being the object of thought does not itself send a signal to the referent. The example separates a semantic relation from a communication channel. It does not prove that thoughts have no neural realization or that empirical inference about them is impossible. In model critique, inspect whether a proposed observable actually measures the relation named in the claim.

## Decision Rules & Judgment

- [M1,M3] If benchmark success is offered as evidence of judgment, ask what shows responsibility for the benchmark's categories and failures.
- [M3] If a human reviewer is proposed, specify what they can inspect, contest, and change. This is an application, not a source-tested oversight protocol.
- [M1] If an explanation equates aboutness with a physical signal, separate the semantic claim from the causal mechanism.
- [M1,M3] If machine judgment is asserted impossible or imminent, preserve Smith's open possibility and return to criteria and evidence.

## Key Takeaways

1. [M1] Representations require worldly and interpretive relationships.
2. [M3] Judgment includes accountability for the frame, not just performance within it.
3. [M1,M3] Reckoning is valuable without being an adequate substitute for every form of judgment.
